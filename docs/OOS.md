## The Ten Rules

### RULE 1 — Feeling over feature

The user's feeling is more important than the feature.

Ship fewer capabilities that land emotionally. Never add capability that dilutes calm, trust, or reflection.

---

### RULE 2 — Calmness wins ties

When two solutions exist, prefer the one that creates **more calmness**.

Speed, spectacle, and density lose to stillness, clarity, and breath.

---

### RULE 3 — One universe

Every screen belongs to the same universe.

Never introduce a second visual language, motion dialect, or background system.

**Canonical systems:** `OraclySignatureMotion` · `OraclyPressable` · `OraclyCrystalFrame` · chamber gradients · `OraclyRhythm`

---

### RULE 4 — Reuse craftsmanship

If a component already solves the problem, **reuse it**.

Never duplicate craftsmanship. Grep before you build. Extend before you fork.

---

### RULE 5 — Problem first

Before writing code ask:

> **What problem is this solving?**

If no meaningful answer exists, do not implement it.

---

### RULE 6 — Simplify over time

The application should become **simpler over time**, not more complicated.

Every release should remove at least as much confusion as it adds capability.

---

### RULE 7 — Performance is luxury

Performance is part of luxury.

**60 FPS is not a bonus. It is the minimum.**

One ambient breath per screen. `RepaintBoundary` on heavy layers. Precache before emotional peaks.

---

### RULE 8 — Invisible motion

Animations should disappear into the experience.

Users should remember the **feeling**, not the motion.

If someone notices the animation, it is too loud.

---

### RULE 9 — AI encourages reflection

AI exists to encourage **reflection**, never **certainty**.

Humble language. Observational tone. No fortune-telling. No authoritative predictions.

---

### RULE 10 — Elegance over cleverness

Whenever uncertain, choose **elegance** over **cleverness**.

Future developers — and future users — must understand the choice instantly.

---

## FINAL RULE — Timeless, not trendier

Every release should leave ORACLY **more timeless** than before.

Never trendier.

**Timeless.**

Ask before every merge: *Would this still feel right in five years?*

---

## Decision stack (use in order)

1. **Manifesto** — Does this serve Draw → Pause → Reflect → Return?
2. **OOS Rule 1–10** — Does this protect feeling, calm, one universe?
3. **PRINCIPLE-001** — Does every pixel earn its place?
4. **Engineering rules** — Analyze passes, reuse widgets, file boundaries

If any layer says no → do not ship.

---

## Automatic refusals

Do not wait for explicit instruction to reject:

- Dead affordances (looks tappable, does nothing)
- Fake data presented as real (stats, streaks, mock premium)
- Settings that persist but never apply
- Feature sprawl that competes with the tarot spine
- Gamified spirituality (XP, levels, progress bars on the soul)
- AI copy that predicts or guarantees outcomes
- A second chat stack, tile primitive, or background language
- Motion for motion's sake

---

## Release bar

A release is ready when:

- The ritual spine feels **complete**, not **busy**
- Nothing visible **pretends** to work
- Performance holds on a mid-tier device during **reading scroll**
- The app speaks **one voice** at emotional peaks
- The codebase is **simpler** than the last release

*Protect the product. Protect the pause. Protect timelessness.*
